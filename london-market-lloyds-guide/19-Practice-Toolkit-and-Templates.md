# Module 19 – Practice Toolkit and Templates

**Purpose:** The working tools for the path in [Module 18](18-World-Class-Consultant-Path.md). Each template is built for repeated practice. Use only **public or fictional data** – never real client data in personal practice or public AI tools.

---

## 1. Syndicate teardown (every two weeks)

Lloyd's publishes annual reports and accounts for each syndicate on lloyds.com **[certain]**. A teardown trains pillar B (financial acumen) faster than anything else.

### 1.1 Method (60–90 minutes)

| Step | What to extract | Where in the accounts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Managing agent, syndicate number, owner group, capacity | Front section / managing agent's report |
| 2 | Gross written premium; growth vs prior year | Managing agent's report, income statement |
| 3 | Business mix by class and territory | Segmental notes |
| 4 | Combined ratio; split into claims ratio and expense ratio | Key performance indicators; technical account |
| 5 | Major/catastrophe losses mentioned | Managing agent's report |
| 6 | Prior-year reserve releases or strengthening | Notes on claims development |
| 7 | Rate change commentary | Managing agent's report |
| 8 | Reinsurance strategy and cost | Report and notes |
| 9 | Investment return | Non-technical/technical account |
| 10 | Principal risks and outlook | Risk section, outlook |

### 1.2 Teardown sheet

```

GWP: £____ (growth __%)        Net earned premium: £____

  of which: major losses __ pts | prior-year releases __ pts

Top 3 classes: ____ / ____ / ____      Top territory: ____
Rate change stated: __%
Reinsurance comment: ____

```

### 1.3 Progression
- Months 1–3: one syndicate at a time; get the numbers right.
- Months 4–9: compare two syndicates writing similar classes; explain the difference.
- Months 9+: compare a managing agent against the Lloyd's market average; write a one-page "board briefing".

## 2. Market news "so-what" note (3 per week)

```
Headline: ____                            Date: ____   Source: ____
FACT (what happened, with numbers): ____
CONFIDENCE: [certain] / [likely] / [guessing]
SO WHAT for:
  - Managing agents: ____
  - Brokers: ____
  - Coverholders/MGAs: ____
  - My spike (AI ops / DA & data): ____
CLIENT QUESTION THIS CREATES: ____
DOES THIS CHANGE ANY POSITION IN MY POVs? Yes/No – how: ____
```

Example (completed):

```
Headline: Lloyd's H1 2026 – risk-adjusted rates down 6.7%     Source: Lloyd's HY2026 release
FACT: CR 90.8%; underlying CR rose to ~84% from ~82%; major claims ratio 6.8%.
CONFIDENCE: [certain] headline; [likely] underlying split
SO WHAT:
  - Managing agents: margins depend more on expense control and selection; plan challenge from Lloyd's likely tougher for 2027.
  - Brokers: more capacity, more facility deployment; pressure on terms.
  - MGAs: carriers will scrutinise binder loss ratios harder.
  - My spike: cost-per-transaction and bordereaux automation business cases strengthen.
CLIENT QUESTION: "Which classes are now below technical price, and what does it cost you to process each risk?"
CHANGES MY POV? Yes – soft-market playbook should lead with expense ratio, not growth.
```

## 3. Practitioner interview guide (20–30 minutes)

Use for network conversations and for engagement discovery.

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Role and context** | What does your team own? What volumes do you handle (risks, bordereaux, claims, transactions) per month? |
| **Process** | Walk me through a typical item from arrival to completion. Where does it wait? Where is it re-keyed? |
| **Pain points** | What breaks most often? What do you spend time on that should not need a person? |
| **Data** | Which data do you trust least? Where does it come from? Who fixes it? |
| **Controls** | What are you most worried an auditor, Lloyd's or the PRA would find? |
| **Change** | What has been tried before? Why did it not stick? |
| **AI** | Where do people already use AI tools informally? Where would you never trust it? |
| **Measures** | Which numbers does your boss look at every month? |
| **Close** | If you had one change funded tomorrow, what would it be? Who else should I speak to? |

**After every conversation:** write five lines – the biggest insight, one number, one quote, one surprise, one follow-up.

## 4. Point-of-view (POV) paper template (2–3 pages)

```
TITLE: a clear position, not a topic
  Weak: "AI in delegated authority"
  Strong: "Bordereaux are a data product, not a report – and AI only helps once you treat them that way"

1. THE ISSUE (half page)
   What is happening in the market, with dated facts and sources.

2. WHY IT MATTERS (half page)
   Impact on P&L, capital, regulation or client service – with numbers or ranges.

3. OUR POSITION (half page)
   The claim. What most firms get wrong. Why.

4. WHAT TO DO (1 page)
   3–5 actions, each with: who owns it, first step, how to measure it, 90-day checkpoint.

5. RISKS AND OBJECTIONS (quarter page)
   The strongest counter-argument and your answer.

6. QUESTIONS FOR YOUR BOARD (quarter page)
   4–5 questions an executive can take into their next meeting.
```

**Quality checks before publishing:** every number sourced and dated; confidence tags on judgements; no vendor promotion; no confidential client information; reviewed by one practitioner.

**Starter POV list (aligned to the recommended spikes):**
1. After Blueprint Two – five no-regret moves for managing agents.
2. Soft-market playbook: protect margin through expense ratio and selection.
3. Bordereaux are a data product, not a report.
4. From AI pilots to production in the Lloyd's market – governance first, then scale.
5. Silent AI: the exposure London has not priced.
6. The real cost of a London market transaction – and how to halve it.

## 5. Value case template

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Baseline | Current volumes, effort (FTE/hours), cost, cycle times, error rates, financial metrics |
| Levers | What changes (automation, process redesign, data standard) and the mechanism |
| Benefits – cost | FTE capacity released, external spend avoided, IT run cost |
| Benefits – financial | Loss ratio, aged debt (cash and credit risk), reinsurance recoveries, capital |
| Benefits – risk and compliance | Fewer breaches, audit findings, regulatory exposure |
| Costs | Build/buy, run, change management, data remediation |
| Assumptions | Each with source and confidence tag |
| Sensitivity | Low / base / high cases |
| Checkpoints | 90-day, 6-month, 12-month measurable milestones |
| Owner | Named business owner of each benefit |

**Rule:** show cost savings and loss ratio/growth benefits separately. CFOs trust the first and discount the second.

## 6. AI sparring partner prompts

Use an AI assistant to rehearse executive conversations and test your understanding. Paste the prompt, then answer as yourself.

**6.1 Executive challenge drill**
```
You are the Chief Operating Officer of a mid-sized Lloyd's managing agent.
You are sceptical of consultants and of AI. Your expense ratio is above peers,
the market is softening, and Lloyd's has retired Blueprint Two.
Interview me for 15 minutes about how I would reduce your operating cost.
Push back hard on vague answers, ask for numbers, and challenge any outdated
market facts. At the end, score me 1-10 on domain credibility, financial
reasoning, and practicality, with specific feedback.
```

**6.2 Concept test**
```
Ask me 10 questions, one at a time, on [topic: e.g., RITC and year of account].
Start easy and get harder. After each answer, tell me what was right, what was
missing, and what an experienced London market practitioner would add.
```

**6.3 POV red team**
```
Here is my point-of-view paper: [paste]. Act as a sceptical Lloyd's CUO.
Find the three weakest claims, any facts that need sources, and the strongest
counter-argument. Do not rewrite it – critique it.
```

**6.4 Process walk-through check**
```
I will describe the London market process for [e.g., bordereaux processing
under a binding authority] step by step. Identify errors, missing steps,
missing controls, and where data quality problems usually arise.
```

**Caution:** AI assistants can be confidently wrong about London market specifics. Check anything important against this guide's sources, Lloyd's publications or a practitioner. Treat the AI as a sparring partner, not an authority.
