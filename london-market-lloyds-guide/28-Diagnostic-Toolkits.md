## Part A – Delegated Authority and Bordereaux Diagnostic

### A1. The question it answers

"How well does the client select, control and learn from its delegated business – and what is it worth to improve?"

### A2. Scope and timeline (4 weeks)

| Week | Activities | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kick-off; data request; interviews (Head of DA, CUO, COO, CFO, claims, compliance, 3–5 DA analysts); process walk-through of one month's bordereaux | Interview notes; process map (P09–P11, P13) |
| 2 | Data analysis: coverholder league table; bordereaux timeliness and quality; GPI use; aged DA premium; breaches; audit findings | Fact base |
| 3 | Scoring of 10 dimensions; coverholder tiering; root causes; value sizing | Scorecard; tiering; value case |
| 4 | Recommendations; roadmap; executive presentation | Board-ready pack |

### A3. Data request

| Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| List of binders: coverholder, class, territory, GPI limit, commission, lead/follow, inception, registration date | Portfolio and control view |
| Three years of risk, premium and claims bordereaux (or summary) | Performance and data quality |
| Bordereaux receipt log (due date, received date, issues) | Timeliness |
| Premium remittance and aged debt by binder | Credit control |
| Audit reports and open findings | Oversight quality |
| Breach log | Authority control |
| DA team structure and time spent by activity | Cost of oversight |
| Binding authority wordings (sample) | Contract and authority quality |
| ATLAS records and annual compliance status | Lloyd's compliance |

### A4. Interview guide (selected questions)

1. How do you decide which coverholders to work with and which to exit?
2. If a top-10 coverholder stopped reporting today, how long before you would know?
3. Are binder terms held as structured data your systems can check against?
4. What share of your team's time is spent mapping and correcting bordereaux?
5. Which coverholders consume most oversight effort, and are they your riskiest?
6. How is coverholder commission linked to profit?

### A5. Core analyses

| Analysis | Method | Typical finding **[guessing]** |
|---|---|---|
| **Coverholder league table** | Loss ratio, growth, premium, data quality, oversight cost by coverholder | Bottom 10% of coverholders drive a large share of losses and effort |
| **Timeliness and quality** | % on time; % valid mandatory fields; trend | Quality unmeasured or below 90% |
| **Authority control** | Risks outside territory/class/limits; GPI breaches | Breaches found weeks after binding |
| **Credit control** | Aged DA premium by binder | Concentrated in a few coverholders |
| **Cost of oversight** | DA FTE and external audit cost ÷ DA premium | Effort spread evenly instead of by risk |
| **Incentives** | Share of commission linked to profit; retained risk | Little profit linkage |

### A6. Scoring (10 dimensions)

Use the **DA diagnostic** sheet: score each dimension 1–5 with evidence. Dimensions and weights: strategy and appetite (8%), partner selection and due diligence (10%), contract and authority (12%), bordereaux timeliness and quality (12%), validation and controls (12%), premium and credit control (8%), claims oversight (10%), performance management and exits (12%), governance and Lloyd's compliance (8%), technology and automation (8%). The sheet gives an overall maturity score and label (Weak, Basic, Developing, Advanced, Leading).

### A7. Value sizing

| Lever | Sizing approach |
|---|---|
| Exit or re-terms of worst coverholders | Loss ratio gap of bottom decile × their premium |
| Profit-linked commission | Expected loss ratio improvement × DA premium |
| Bordereaux automation | FTE effort removed × cost (see [Module 11 §5.3](11-Consulting-Playbook.md)) |
| Faster exposure data | Qualitative; reinsurance and capital accuracy |
| Credit control | Aged debt reduction; credit risk |

### A8. Output and follow-on work

**Output:** maturity scorecard; coverholder league table and tiers; top five issues with value; 12-month roadmap.
**Follow-on engagements:** DA operating model redesign; contract register and bordereaux platform; coverholder portfolio remediation; DA data standard readiness.

## Part B – AI Readiness Diagnostic

### B1. The question it answers

"Is the client ready to scale AI where it creates value – and what must change first?"

### B2. Scope and timeline (4 weeks)

| Week | Activities | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Interviews (CEO, COO, CUO, CDO/CIO, CRO, claims, DA, compliance); inventory of current AI use (approved and informal) | AI inventory; ambition |
| 2 | Process walk-throughs to find document-heavy steps ([Module 25](25-Lloyds-IT-Landscape-Swimlanes.md)); data samples (submissions, bordereaux, claims files) | Use-case long list; data readiness |
| 3 | Score 9 dimensions; prioritise use cases (value × feasibility × risk); governance gap review vs LMA toolkit principles and SM&CR | Scorecard; priority list |
| 4 | Roadmap; first proof of value design (test set and metrics); executive presentation | Board-ready AI plan |

### B3. Data request

| Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| AI policy, inventory, risk register | Governance |
| List of AI pilots and results | Portfolio maturity |
| Samples: 50–200 submissions, 10 bordereaux, 20 claims files (anonymised) | Data readiness and quick proof |
| Systems landscape | Integration feasibility |
| Data quality metrics (if any) | Foundations |
| Training records | Skills |

### B4. Scoring (9 dimensions)

Use the **AI readiness** sheet: strategy and value (12%), use-case portfolio (10%), data foundations (16%), workflow integration (12%), governance and risk (14%), evaluation and monitoring (12%), skills and change (10%), technology and vendors (8%), broker and market alignment (6%).

### B5. Use-case prioritisation

Use the **AI use-case priority** sheet with use cases from [Module 26](26-AI-Use-Cases-Capabilities-Live-Examples.md). Priority = 50% value + 30% feasibility + 20% (6 − risk). The example scores rank **submission intake** and **bordereaux mapping and validation** first, which matches market evidence that intake and extraction are where brokers see most value today **[likely]**.

### B6. Output and follow-on work

**Output:** readiness scorecard; prioritised use cases; governance gaps; 90-day proof of value plan with test set and success criteria; 12-month roadmap.
**Follow-on engagements:** proof of value delivery; AI governance set-up; data foundations; scaling programme.

## Part C – Coverholder tiering tool

The **Coverholder tiering** sheet scores each coverholder on six factors (premium size, class risk, loss ratio vs plan, data quality and timeliness, audit findings, conduct exposure) with weights, and assigns Tier 1, 2 or 3 with matching oversight intensity. Example rows are clearly labelled; replace them with client data.

## Part D – Partner's lens

1. **Sell the diagnostic, deliver the insight, earn the programme.** A four-week diagnostic is easy to approve; a clear value case earns the transformation.
2. **Score with evidence only.** Every score needs a document, data point or interview quote. Unsupported scores lose the room.
3. **Compare across clients.** After three diagnostics, anonymised score distributions become your own benchmark – another owned asset.
4. **Put a number on every finding.** "Data quality is weak" is an opinion; "18% of bordereaux rows fail validation, costing 6 FTE" is a finding.
