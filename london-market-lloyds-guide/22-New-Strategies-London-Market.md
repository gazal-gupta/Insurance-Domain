## 1. How to read this chapter

Each strategy is described with the same seven points: **what it is**, **evidence**, **economics**, **who benefits**, **risks**, **maturity**, and **consulting angle**. Section 5 gives a framework to choose between them for a specific client.

## 2. Strategies led by Lloyd's (the Corporation)

### S1 – Cut the cost and friction of doing business at Lloyd's

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Reduce the cost and time of operating at Lloyd's for managing agents and capital providers |
| **Evidence** | 2026–2030 strategy: "efficient and flexible marketplace"; ambition of a **1% incremental cost of operating at Lloyd's** **[likely]**; in November 2026 Lloyd's combined its oversight functions into one division as a "first step in eliminating unnecessary friction and complexity" **[likely]**; LMA secured annual change cycles for the Principles for Business Oversight **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | Lower central charges and lower internal compliance cost for managing agents |
| **Who benefits** | Managing agents, capital providers, new entrants |
| **Risks** | Simplification may be slower than hoped; performance expectations may rise in return |
| **Maturity** | Early implementation |
| **Consulting angle** | Help managing agents redesign their Lloyd's-facing reporting and oversight processes; quantify the "cost of Lloyd's" and track benefits |

### S2 – Risk-based, predictable oversight

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Oversight intensity tailored to each managing agent's performance and maturity; predictable change cycles |
| **Evidence** | Strategy commitments to predictable, risk-based oversight and streamlined reporting **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | Good performers face less burden; weak performers face more |
| **Consulting angle** | "Oversight readiness" – self-assessment against principles; evidence packs; MI that shows Lloyd's what it needs |

### S3 – Make Lloyd's the most capital-efficient marketplace

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Attract institutional and private capital; simplify Names processes; tailor oversight for different capital types; target return on capital above 12% across a 10-year cycle |
| **Evidence** | Strategy driver "maximised capital advantage"; Names simplification; London Bridge 2 growth; capital-backed syndicates **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | More capital; lower cost of capital; more capacity in hard markets |
| **Risks** | More passive capital adds to soft-market pressure |
| **Consulting angle** | Capital structuring; investor reporting; capital efficiency reviews |

### S4 – Incremental modernisation and open architecture

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Move from a single market cutover (Blueprint Two) to incremental change, common data standards and open architecture |
| **Evidence** | March 2026 decision; Velonetic refocused; CDR continues **[likely]** |
| **Consulting angle** | Firm-level roadmaps aligned to standards ([Module 08](08-Advanced-Modernisation-Data-Technology.md)) |

### S5 – Innovation through Lloyd's Lab

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Accelerator for insurtechs on themes Lloyd's wants to develop |
| **Evidence** | Cohort 16 (2026) themes included operational efficiency, new products and emerging risks such as AI and battery assets; Cohort 17 (September 2026) included a "portfolio steering and capital allocation" theme **[likely]** |
| **Consulting angle** | Scouting and piloting Lab solutions for clients; building business cases for adoption |

## 3. Strategies of carriers and managing agents

### S6 – Scale through consolidation

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Global groups acquire London specialty franchises; mid-size players merge or sell |
| **Evidence** | Zurich completed its £8.1bn acquisition of Beazley on 1 October 2026 (combined specialty business about $15bn GWP) **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | Scale in distribution, data, reinsurance buying and capital; cost synergies |
| **Risks** | Talent loss; culture clash; integration complexity; Lloyd's and regulatory approvals |
| **Consulting angle** | Due diligence; integration management office; platform choice (Lloyd's vs company); underwriter retention |

### S7 – Build a lead franchise

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Invest in specialist underwriters, claims excellence and data to lead more business |
| **Evidence** | Lloyd's strategy driver "leading underwriting performance" **[likely]**; facility growth squeezes followers **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | Leads set price and terms, see more risks first and attract follow capital |
| **Risks** | Expensive talent; requires patience in a soft market |
| **Consulting angle** | Lead share analytics; underwriting talent strategy; claims as a differentiator |

### S8 – Smart follow: data-driven and algorithmic following

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Deploy follow capacity using data and rules across many risks, selecting which leads and segments to follow |
| **Evidence** | Algorithmic follow platforms such as Ki (syndicate 1618) and tracker-style syndicates **[likely]**; AI-assisted syndicate 2479 from 2026 **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | Very low cost per risk; scalable capacity |
| **Risks** | Adverse selection in a soft market; model risk; dependence on lead quality |
| **Consulting angle** | Lead-quality scoring; follow portfolio analytics; model governance |

### S9 – AI-native underwriting and operations

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Rebuild underwriting and operations around AI: intake, triage, pricing support, bordereaux, claims |
| **Evidence** | LMA survey and AI Adoption Toolkit; AI-assisted syndicates **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | Lower expense ratio; better selection; faster quotes |
| **Risks** | Governance, accuracy, over-automation, regulatory scrutiny |
| **Consulting angle** | AI strategy, governance and scaling ([Module 09](09-Advanced-AI-in-London-Market.md)) |

### S10 – Dual and multi-platform optimisation

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Choose for each class the best platform: Lloyd's syndicate, UK company, Bermuda, US E&S carrier, EU entity |
| **Evidence** | Hybrid groups already run multiple platforms; consolidation increases the choice **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | Lower cost and capital; better distribution access |
| **Consulting angle** | Platform economics model per class (licences, rating, capital, cost, tax) |

### S11 – Distribution partnerships and MGA platforms

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Carriers build or buy MGAs, incubate new MGAs, or form multi-year capacity partnerships |
| **Evidence** | MGAs and Lloyd's brokers reached a record 29% of UK distribution deals in 2026; private equity active in specialty distribution **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | Access to distribution and specialist talent; fee income |
| **Risks** | Misaligned incentives; carriers pulling back from MGA exposure as markets soften **[likely]** |
| **Consulting angle** | MGA partnership design; alignment terms; DA oversight |

### S12 – Cost transformation through operating model change

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Shared services, global capability centres, automation, application rationalisation |
| **Evidence** | Soft market pressure; Lloyd's cost focus **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | 1–2 points of expense ratio over two years **[guessing]** |
| **Consulting angle** | Operating model design; location strategy; automation programme ([Module 10 Case 8](10-Business-Scenarios-and-Case-Studies.md)) |

### S13 – Capital efficiency and legacy clean-up

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Buy cheap reinsurance, use ADCs and LPTs on older casualty years, close open years, optimise mix |
| **Evidence** | Soft reinsurance pricing; social inflation reserve pressure **[likely]** |
| **Economics** | Released capital; higher return on capital ([Module 07](07-Advanced-Capital-Finance-Regulation.md)) |
| **Consulting angle** | Capital efficiency reviews; legacy transaction support |

### S14 – Claims as a competitive weapon

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What** | Differentiate on claims speed, expertise and certainty; use claims data in underwriting |
| **Evidence** | Lloyd's strategy targets top-tier client satisfaction (NPS) **[likely]**; brokers choose leads partly on claims reputation |
| **Consulting angle** | Claims transformation; leakage; triage; client experience measures |

## 6. Choosing strategies for a client

### 6.1 Attractiveness × ability to win

| | Low ability to win | High ability to win |
|---|---|---|
| **High attractiveness** | Partner or acquire capability | **Invest now** |
| **Low attractiveness** | Avoid | Maintain or harvest |

**Attractiveness factors:** profit pool size, growth, cycle resilience, capital intensity, regulatory direction.
**Ability-to-win factors:** underwriting talent, lead position, distribution access, data and technology, capital, brand, cost position.

### 6.3 Questions for a strategy refresh workshop

1. Where are we a **lead** and where are we a **passenger**?
2. Which classes are below technical price, and what will we stop doing?
3. What would a competitor with twice our scale do with our book?
4. Which partner (broker, MGA, capital provider) would hurt most if it left – and why would it stay?
5. What two capabilities (talent, data, AI, capital) would most change our ability to win?
6. Which of the 23 strategies are we pursuing by accident rather than by choice?

## 8. Partner's lens

1. **Strategy is choosing what not to do.** In a soft market, the most valuable strategic choice is often what to stop writing, which partners to exit and which capabilities not to build.
2. **Separate fashion from fundamentals.** AI, facilities and parametric are fashionable; the fundamentals are still selection, price, cost, capital and distribution. Test every new strategy against them.
3. **Follow the profit pool.** Ask where profit is moving (towards leads, distribution, scale and data) and whether the client is positioned to capture it.
4. **Pair every strategy with an operating model.** A strategy without the people, processes, data and governance to deliver it is a slogan. Top-tier work always ends in "what changes on Monday".
5. **Track the signals quarterly.** Use the signposts in [Module 20 §6](20-Market-Landscape-Trends-Outlook-2027-2030.md) to know when a strategy's assumptions stop holding.

---

## 9. Key takeaways

1. Lloyd's is pursuing lower cost and friction, risk-based oversight, capital efficiency and incremental modernisation.
2. Carriers are choosing between scale (consolidation), lead franchise, smart follow, AI-native operations and cost transformation.
3. Distribution power is shifting to brokers, facilities and MGAs; carriers respond with partnerships and selectivity.
4. New risk classes, parametric products and international hubs offer growth with new risks.
5. Use attractiveness × ability to win, and always pair strategy with an operating model.
