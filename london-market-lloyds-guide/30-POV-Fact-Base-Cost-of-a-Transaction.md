## 2. The question and working thesis

**Key question:** How much does it cost the London market to place, process, settle and claim on one contract – where does that cost sit, and who could remove it?

**Working thesis [guessing] – to prove or disprove with your analysis:**
1. The largest controllable cost is not the bureau or Lloyd's charges but **repeated handling of the same data** across broker, platform, carriers, coverholders and the bureau.
2. Because subscription spreads one contract across many carriers, **each carrier pays to process the same contract** – a market-level cost no single firm can remove alone.
3. AI and data standards can remove a large share of it, but **only firms that fix data at intake** capture the benefit; brokers are moving first.

## 4. Original analysis you must add (this is what makes it yours)

| # | Analysis | Method | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Touches per contract** | Trace 10–20 real contracts (open market and DA) at a client or with practitioner help; count every manual entry of the same data | "Each contract's core data is entered X times across Y systems" |
| A2 | **Cost per transaction** | Activity analysis in one or two operations teams (time per premium entry, endorsement, claim movement, bordereau) × loaded cost | £ per transaction type (ranges) |
| A3 | **Expense ratio decomposition** | Extend the benchmark to 30+ syndicates; compare expense ratios by business model (lead-heavy, DA-heavy, follow/tracker, reinsurance) | Exhibit of expense ratio by model |
| A4 | **Market-wide cost pool** | Combine A2 with market volumes (estimate transparently) | Range of annual cost across the market |
| A5 | **Practitioner pulse** | 15–20 short interviews (COO, Head of DA, technical accounting leads, brokers) | Quotes and patterns |
| A6 | **Removable share** | Classify cost drivers: removable by firm alone / needs counterparties / needs market standard | "Who pays and who can fix it" matrix |

**Rule:** state your method and ranges openly. A transparent estimate with ranges beats a precise number nobody can check.

## 5. Storyline (pyramid) – structure for you to write

**Governing thought (draft, to be replaced by your evidence-based version):** *The London market pays far more to move data than to carry risk; the firms that capture data once, at intake, will take share as margins tighten.*

| Key line | Evidence to use | Exhibit |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The cost of a transaction is driven by repeated handling, not by any single system | A1, A2, F11 | Flow of one contract with every re-entry marked |

| 3. The soft market turns this from an operations issue into a strategic one | F1, F2, F5, F6, A3 | Expense ratio by business model vs combined ratio |

**Situation – Complication – Question (opening):**
- *Situation:* strong recent results.
- *Complication:* falling rates, rising underlying ratios, broker technology raising the bar, Blueprint Two retired.
- *Question:* where can managing agents take out cost without hurting underwriting?

## 7. Objections to prepare for

| Objection | Your answer (build from evidence) |
|---|---|
| "London's cost is the price of complex risk." | Separate judgement cost (underwriting, claims expertise) from handling cost (re-entry, reconciliation); only the second is in scope |
| "The bureau and Lloyd's are the problem." | Show the share of cost inside firms' own control (A6) |
| "AI will fix it, so why bother with standards?" | Show AI accuracy depends on structured inputs; evidence from bordereaux tools and intake use cases |
| "Your numbers are estimates." | Agree; show method and ranges; invite firms to join next year's study |

---

## 8. Review checklist before publishing

- Every number has a source and date; vendor and company claims are attributed as claims.
- Confidence tags on judgements.
- No confidential client data; anonymised examples only.
- Answer-first: the governing thought appears in the first paragraph.
- One practitioner (operations or DA) and one senior consultant have reviewed it.
- Plain English; no jargon without a short explanation.
- Clear call to action: what a COO should do in the next 90 days.
