## 1. Why this chapter matters

Each class of business is almost a separate industry, with its own data, models, wordings, brokers and experts. A consultant who treats "London specialty" as one thing gives generic advice. A consultant who knows that a hull underwriter thinks about vessel age and trading area, while a cyber underwriter thinks about technology dependency and aggregation, gives advice that lands.

For each class, this chapter covers the same eight points: **what it covers, who buys, how it is placed, key data, key wordings, pricing, typical claims, and the 2026 agenda**.

## 3. Marine

### 3.1 Hull and machinery (H&M)

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Covers** | Physical loss of or damage to ships (collision, grounding, fire, heavy weather); collision liability (often three-fourths in English hull clauses); general average contributions |
| **Buyers** | Shipowners and operators; banks financing ships (as co-assureds or loss payees) |
| **Placement** | Mostly open market subscription; large fleets led by specialist marine underwriters; Nordic market competes |
| **Key data** | Fleet list with IMO numbers, vessel type, age, flag, classification society, tonnage, values, trading areas, claims history, management company |
| **Key wordings** | Institute Time Clauses (Hulls), International Hull Clauses, Nordic Marine Insurance Plan **[certain]** |
| **Pricing** | Rate on agreed value, adjusted for fleet size, age, type, claims record, deductibles |
| **Typical claims** | Machinery breakdown (frequent), groundings, collisions, fires on car carriers and container ships |
| **2026 agenda [likely]** | Fires on large vessels (car carriers carrying electric vehicles); sanctions compliance for vessels in the "shadow fleet"; ageing fleet; values rising with newbuild costs |

### 3.2 Cargo

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Covers** | Goods in transit by sea, air, road and rail, including storage incidental to transit; often on an annual **open cover** for all shipments |
| **Buyers** | Traders, manufacturers, commodity houses, retailers, freight forwarders |
| **Placement** | Open covers via open market or binders; large commodity traders placed by specialist brokers |
| **Key data** | Commodities, packing, routes, conveyances, annual shipped values, stock storage locations and values |
| **Key wordings** | Institute Cargo Clauses (A) all risks, (B) and (C) named perils **[certain]** |
| **Pricing** | Rate per £100 of shipped value; stock throughput policies priced on values at risk in storage |
| **Typical claims** | Theft, water damage, temperature failure (reefers), general average, port explosions or fires |
| **2026 agenda [likely]** | Port and warehouse **accumulation** (one event hitting many cargo policies); supply-chain disruption; war and strikes cover in conflict zones |

### 3.3 Marine liability and P&I reinsurance

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Covers** | Liabilities of ports, terminals, charterers, ship repairers, freight forwarders. Shipowners' third-party liability (P&I) is mostly insured by mutual **P&I Clubs**, whose large losses are pooled and reinsured – heavily in London **[certain]** |
| **Typical claims** | Cargo liability, injury, pollution, wreck removal, damage to fixed objects (e.g., bridges) |
| **Real example** | The 2024 Baltimore bridge collapse involving the *Dali* – a large P&I and liability loss **[certain]** |

### 3.4 Marine war, specie and others

| Sub-class | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Marine war** | War, strikes, terrorism, piracy, confiscation. Additional premium for voyages into **listed areas** set by the Joint War Committee **[certain]**; notice of cancellation provisions allow quick re-pricing |
| **Specie** | Cash in transit, vaults, precious metals, fine art and jewellery in transit and storage |
| **Yacht** | Private and commercial yachts |

**Marine – consulting questions:** "How do you track vessel movements against sanctions and listed areas in real time?" "How do you measure cargo accumulation at major ports?"

## 4. Aviation and space

| Sub-class | Covers | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Airline hull and liability** | Aircraft damage; passenger and third-party liability | Very large limits; few large leads; fleet data and safety record |
| **Aviation war** | War, hijack, confiscation – excluded from all risks by the AVN 48B clause and written back in war cover **[certain]** | Separate panel from all risks |
| **Lessors' contingent / possessed cover** | Protects lessors if the airline's cover fails or aircraft are not returned | Central to the Russia litigation |
| **Aerospace products** | Liability of manufacturers and component makers | Long-tail product liability |
| **Airports and general aviation** | Airport liability; smaller aircraft | Smaller, more binder business |
| **Space** | Launch, in-orbit satellite risks | Few risks, huge values; cyclical |

**Real case [likely]:** in June 2025 the High Court found lessors could recover under **war risks** cover for aircraft stuck in Russia; AerCap recovered just over $1bn; the Court of Appeal granted insurers permission to appeal in March 2026. Lesson: which panel pays depends on wording and cause.

**2026 agenda [likely]:** litigation reserves; reinsurance disputes; pricing of war covers; satellite constellations' accumulation.

## 5. Energy

| Sub-class | Covers | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Upstream** | Offshore platforms, rigs, pipelines; **control of well / operators' extra expense (OEE)**; construction | Engineering surveys; cat exposure (Gulf of Mexico windstorm) |
| **Downstream** | Refineries, petrochemical plants – property damage and business interruption | Engineering surveys; large BI values; maximum foreseeable loss estimates |
| **Power and renewables** | Power stations, onshore and offshore wind, solar, battery storage, hydrogen | New technologies; limited loss history; serial losses from component defects |
| **Energy liability** | Pollution, third-party liability | Long-tail |

**2026 agenda [likely]:** energy transition (balancing fossil fuel exposure policies with renewables growth); serial defects in wind turbines; cyber exposure of industrial control systems; war and political violence for energy infrastructure.

**Consulting question:** "How do you price new energy technologies with little loss history, and how do you aggregate serial defect risk?"

## 6. Property

### 6.1 Direct and facultative (D&F) property

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Covers** | Large commercial and industrial property damage and business interruption; facultative reinsurance of individual large risks |
| **Buyers** | Corporations, real estate owners, manufacturers; insurers buying facultative cover |
| **Placement** | Open market subscription, layered towers; growing use of broker facilities **[likely]** |
| **Key data** | **Statement of values (SOV)**: each location's address (geocoded), construction, occupancy, protection, exposure (**COPE**), values for buildings, contents, BI |
| **Pricing** | Cat models for natural perils; attritional rating; layer pricing (rate on line) |
| **Typical claims** | Fire, explosion, windstorm, flood, earthquake, wildfire; BI from supply chain |
| **2026 agenda [likely]** | Rates falling after strong years; **secondary perils** (convective storms, wildfire, flood) growing; SOV data quality; valuation adequacy (under-insurance after inflation) |

### 6.2 US E&S property through binders

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Covers** | Small commercial and residential property in hard-to-place US areas (coastal, wildfire) |
| **Placement** | Delegated authority: coverholders bind locally; bordereaux report risks |
| **Key data** | Risk bordereaux with locations, construction, values, deductibles |
| **2026 agenda [likely]** | Cat aggregation from DA data quality; competition from US domestic E&S carriers; softening rates |

### 6.3 Terrorism and political violence

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Covers** | Terrorism, sabotage, strikes, riots, civil commotion, insurrection, war (in political violence) |
| **Context** | State schemes in some countries (e.g., Pool Re in the UK, TRIA backstop in the US) **[certain]**; London writes stand-alone covers globally |
| **2026 agenda [likely]** | Civil unrest; conflict zones; aggregation in city centres |

### 6.4 Business interruption

Loss of gross profit or revenue after insured physical damage; contingent BI for supplier or customer damage. The COVID-19 BI disputes showed how wording precision drives outcomes.

**Property – consulting questions:** "What share of your SOV is geocoded to rooftop level?" "What is your view on secondary perils, and is it in your pricing?"

## 7. Casualty and liability

| Sub-class | Covers | Trigger | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **General liability (GL)** | Bodily injury and property damage to third parties | Usually occurrence | US casualty is a large London market; excess layers above US primaries |
| **Professional indemnity (PI) / E&O** | Errors by professionals (lawyers, accountants, technology firms, engineers) | Claims made | Retroactive dates; aggregation of related claims |
| **Directors & officers (D&O)** | Claims against directors and managers | Claims made | Side A/B/C; securities class actions; "AI washing" claims emerging **[likely]** |
| **Financial institutions (FI)** | Bankers' blanket bond (crime), FI PI, FI D&O | Claims made / discovery | Fraud; cyber crime; deepfake payment fraud **[likely]** |
| **Medical malpractice** | Hospitals and practitioners | Claims made | Long tail; jurisdiction-specific |
| **Cyber** | Data breach costs, ransomware, business interruption from IT outages, liability | Claims made / event | Systemic risk; war and state-backed attack exclusions **[certain]** |

### 7.1 Key casualty concepts

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Long tail** | Claims reported and settled years after the policy period |
| **Social inflation** | Rising US claims costs from large jury awards, litigation funding and legal trends – the main reserve risk for US casualty **[certain]** |
| **Retroactive date** | Claims-made policies cover only acts after this date |
| **Extended reporting period** | Time after expiry to report claims |
| **Aggregation clauses** | Whether related claims count as one claim (one limit and one deductible) |

### 7.2 Cyber in more detail

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **First-party covers** | Incident response, data restoration, business interruption, extortion |
| **Third-party covers** | Privacy liability, network security liability, regulatory defence |
| **Exclusions** | Since 31 March 2023 Lloyd's requires exclusions for state-backed cyber attacks using approved clauses (e.g., LMA5564–5567) **[certain]**; infrastructure and war exclusions |
| **Systemic risk** | One event (software flaw, cloud outage, widespread malware) can hit thousands of insureds |
| **Data** | Revenue, sector, security controls, technology dependencies |
| **2026 agenda [likely]** | Soft pricing; ransomware frequency; aggregation modelling; AI-enabled attacks |

**Casualty – consulting questions:** "How are you reflecting social inflation in pricing and older-year reserves?" "How do you measure systemic cyber accumulation, including silent cyber in other classes?"

## 8. Specialty

| Sub-class | Covers | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Political risk** | Confiscation, expropriation, nationalisation and deprivation (CEND); currency inconvertibility; contract frustration; political violence | Country risk; long policy periods; sanctions |
| **Credit and surety** | Trade credit; structured credit; surety bonds | Linked to economic cycle; bank capital relief deals |
| **Kidnap and ransom (K&R)** | Ransom, crisis consultants, extortion | Confidentiality; specialist response firms |
| **Contingency** | Event cancellation, non-appearance, weather | COVID-19 losses reshaped wordings (communicable disease exclusions) |
| **Fine art, jewellery, specie** | High-value assets | Valuation; security |
| **Bloodstock** | Racehorses and breeding stock | Mortality and fertility |
| **Accident and health (A&H)** | Personal accident, travel, sports disability | Often via coverholders and affinity programmes |
| **Product recall and contamination** | Recall costs, loss of profit | Food and pharma |
| **AI liability (emerging)** | Affirmative cover for AI model errors – e.g., Armilla AI, Testudo **[likely]** | New class; silent AI elsewhere |

**Specialty – consulting question:** "Where are you carrying unpriced AI or political exposure in existing wordings?"

## 10. Reinsurance in depth

### 10.1 Structures

| Type | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Treaty** | Covers a whole portfolio automatically | Quota share of a cedant's US property book |
| **Facultative** | One risk, negotiated individually | Reinsuring one large refinery |
| **Proportional** | Premium and losses shared in the same proportion; reinsurer pays ceding commission | Quota share (fixed %), surplus (by lines above retention) |
| **Non-proportional (XoL)** | Pays losses above a retention up to a limit | Per-risk XoL; cat XoL $100m xs $50m |
| **Aggregate / stop loss** | Protects annual aggregate losses or loss ratio | Pays if loss ratio exceeds 75% |
| **Retrocession** | Reinsurance of reinsurers | |
| **Alternative capital** | Cat bonds, collateralised reinsurance, sidecars, industry loss warranties | Lloyd's London Bridge vehicle for ILS **[certain]** |
| **Legacy** | Loss portfolio transfer, adverse development cover, RITC | |

### 10.2 Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cedant | Insurer buying reinsurance |
| Ceding commission | Payment from reinsurer to cedant on proportional treaties |
| Reinstatement | Restoring a used limit, often for extra premium |
| Hours clause | Time window defining one "event" (e.g., 72 hours for windstorm) |
| Ultimate net loss (UNL) | Cedant's loss after other recoveries, used to measure XoL losses |
| Losses occurring during (LOD) vs risks attaching during (RAD) | Trigger basis of a treaty |
| Rate on line (ROL) | Premium ÷ limit |
| Payback | Years of premium needed to pay one full limit loss (1 ÷ ROL) |

### 10.3 Renewal calendar **[certain]**

| Date | Market |
|---|---|
| 1 January | Europe, global programmes, much of London |
| 1 April | Japan and some Asia |
| 1 June | Florida property catastrophe |
| 1 July | US, Australia, New Zealand |

### 10.4 Reinsurance market in 2026 **[likely]**

Plentiful capital and lighter recent catastrophe years are pushing property catastrophe prices down; casualty reinsurance remains focused on reserve adequacy for older US years. For buyers this is an opportunity to buy more protection at lower cost; for sellers, a margin squeeze.

## 14. Partner's lens

*How a senior top-tier consultant would use this chapter.*

1. **Segment before you analyse.** Never analyse a London portfolio as one block. The first split is usually by class; the second by channel (open market, DA, facility) or lead/follow. Many "portfolio problems" are one or two segments.
2. **Know each class's profit driver.** Property is driven by cat and selection; casualty by reserving and social inflation; marine by fleet quality and sanctions; cyber by aggregation; reinsurance by attachment and terms. Your recommendation must address the driver of that class.
3. **Learn the class language fast.** In the first week of a class-specific engagement, learn ten terms that class's underwriters use daily. Credibility follows immediately.
4. **Look for cross-class accumulation.** Partners ask the question no single class asks: "What single event hits several of your classes at once?" (a port explosion hits cargo, property, marine liability and reinsurance). That question often leads to the most valuable work.

---

## 15. Key takeaways

1. Each class is a different business with its own data, wordings, pricing and claims patterns.
2. Property and reinsurance are driven by catastrophe and selection; casualty by reserving and social inflation; cyber by systemic aggregation.
3. Marine, aviation and energy depend on specialist expertise, sanctions compliance and precise wordings.
4. Placement channel differs by class, which shapes the operational problems you will see.
5. In 2026, softening rates affect most classes; terms discipline and cross-class accumulation are key themes.
