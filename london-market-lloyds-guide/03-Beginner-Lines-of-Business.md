# Module 03 – Lines of Business and Products

**Level:** Beginner | **Goal:** Know what London insures, the key risks and data in each class, and the typical client problems per class. Clients will expect you to know the difference between, for example, hull and cargo, or treaty and facultative.

---

## 1. How Lloyd's groups its business

Lloyd's reports its business in these segments **[certain]** (names used in Lloyd's annual reports):

| Segment | What it includes |
|---|---|
| **Reinsurance** | Property treaty, casualty treaty, specialty treaty |
| **Property** | Direct and facultative (D&F) property, binders, terrorism, political violence |
| **Casualty** | General liability, professional indemnity, D&O, financial institutions, medical malpractice, cyber |
| **Marine, Aviation & Transport (MAT)** | Hull, cargo, marine liability, specie, war, aviation, space |
| **Energy** | Upstream, downstream, power, renewables, energy liability |
| **Motor** | UK and overseas motor (smaller) |
| **Life** | Term life and personal accident (small) |

Property, reinsurance and casualty together make up the large majority of Lloyd's premium **[likely]**.

## 2. Class-by-class guide

### 2.1 Marine

| Sub-class | What it covers | Key data and terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Hull & Machinery (H&M)** | Physical damage to ships. | Vessel IMO number, flag, class society, age, tonnage, trading area. Institute Time Clauses (Hulls), Nordic Plan. |
| **Cargo** | Goods in transit (sea, air, road). Often on an **open cover** for all shipments of a client in a year. | Commodity, conveyance, voyage, values, storage. Institute Cargo Clauses (A), (B), (C). |
| **Marine liability** | Liabilities of ports, terminals, charterers, ship repairers. | Throughput, contracts. |
| **P&I reinsurance** | Shipowner liability is mainly covered by mutual **P&I Clubs**; London reinsures the International Group's pooling agreement. | Large single losses, e.g., Baltimore bridge collapse (2024). |
| **Marine war** | War, strikes, terrorism, piracy; often on **listed areas** set by the Joint War Committee (JWC). | Listed areas, notice of cancellation, breach additional premium. |
| **Specie** | High value goods: cash in transit, fine art, jewellery, vaults. | Values, security, transit. |

**Typical client problem:** sanctions and war risk monitoring (Red Sea, Black Sea, Russia oil price cap), accumulation of cargo in ports.

### 2.2 Aviation and space

| Sub-class | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Airline hull and liability** | Aircraft damage and passenger/third-party liability. |
| **Aviation war** | War, hijack, confiscation – AVN 48B exclusion and write-back. |
| **Lessors' contingent / possessed cover** | Protects aircraft lessors when the airline's cover fails – the Russia stranded aircraft dispute. |
| **General aviation, aerospace products, airports** | Smaller aircraft, manufacturers, airport liability. |
| **Space** | Launch, in-orbit satellite risk. |

**Case to know:** In June 2025 the High Court found that lessors (AerCap and others) could recover under **war risks** cover for aircraft stuck in Russia, not under all risks **[certain]**. The Court of Appeal gave insurers permission to appeal in March 2026 **[likely]**. Lesson: the exact wording and the cause of loss (war vs non-war) decides which insurers pay.

### 2.3 Energy

| Sub-class | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Upstream** | Offshore platforms, drilling rigs, control of well (OEE – Operators Extra Expense), construction. |
| **Downstream** | Refineries, petrochemical plants – property damage and business interruption. |
| **Power and renewables** | Power generation, wind farms, solar, battery storage. |
| **Energy liability** | Pollution, third-party liability. |

**Trend:** Energy transition – insurers balancing fossil fuel exits (ESG commitments) with new renewable risks (offshore wind, hydrogen, carbon capture) **[likely]**.

### 2.4 Property

| Sub-class | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Direct & Facultative (D&F)** | Large commercial property risks (open market) and facultative reinsurance of individual risks. |
| **US E&S property binders** | Smaller US commercial and residential property written by coverholders (delegated authority). Major driver of Lloyd's US property book. |
| **Terrorism & political violence** | Stand-alone terrorism, sabotage, riots, civil war. |
| **Business interruption (BI)** | Loss of profit after physical damage. |

**Key data:** Statement of Values (SOV) – a schedule of locations with addresses, construction, occupancy, protection, exposure (COPE) and values. Geocoding quality drives catastrophe model output.

### 2.5 Casualty / liability

| Sub-class | Covers |
|---|---|
| **General liability (GL)** | Bodily injury and property damage to third parties. US casualty is a big London market. |
| **Professional indemnity (PI) / E&O** | Errors by professionals (lawyers, accountants, tech firms). |
| **Directors & Officers (D&O)** | Claims against directors and managers. |
| **Financial institutions (FI)** | Bankers' blanket bond (crime), FI PI, FI D&O. |
| **Medical malpractice** | Hospitals and practitioners. |
| **Cyber** | Data breach, ransomware, business interruption from IT outages, liability. London is one of the largest cyber markets globally **[likely]**. |

**Key concept – long-tail:** casualty claims may take many years to be reported and settled. Reserving uncertainty and **social inflation** (rising US jury awards and litigation funding) are big themes **[certain]**.

**Cyber exclusions:** Since 31 March 2023, Lloyd's requires cyber policies to exclude losses from **state-backed cyber attacks** using approved clauses (e.g., LMA 5564–5567) **[certain]**.

### 2.6 Specialty

| Sub-class | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Political risk** | Confiscation, expropriation, nationalisation, deprivation (CEND), currency inconvertibility, contract frustration. |
| **Credit and surety** | Trade credit, structured credit, surety bonds. |
| **Kidnap & Ransom (K&R)** | Ransom payments, crisis response. |
| **Contingency** | Event cancellation, non-appearance, weather. |
| **Fine art, jewellery, bloodstock** | High-value niche assets (bloodstock = racehorses). |
| **Accident & Health (A&H)** | Personal accident, travel, sports disability. |
| **Product recall, crisis management** | Contamination, recall costs. |
| **AI liability (emerging)** | Affirmative cover for AI model errors – e.g., products from Armilla and Testudo backed by Lloyd's capacity **[likely]**. |

### 2.7 Reinsurance

| Type | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Treaty** | Covers a whole portfolio of the cedant's business automatically. | Quota share of a cedant's US property book. |
| **Facultative** | Covers one specific risk, negotiated individually. | Reinsuring one large refinery. |
| **Proportional (pro-rata)** | Reinsurer shares premium and losses in a fixed proportion. Pays **ceding commission** to cedant. | Quota share (fixed %), Surplus (based on line size). |
| **Non-proportional (excess of loss – XoL)** | Reinsurer pays losses above a retention, up to a limit. | Cat XoL: $100m xs $50m per event. Per-risk XoL. |
| **Stop loss / aggregate** | Protects the cedant's total annual loss ratio or aggregate losses. | Pays if loss ratio > 75%. |
| **Retrocession** | Reinsurance of reinsurers. | |
| **Alternative capital / ILS** | Catastrophe bonds, collateralised reinsurance, sidecars, industry loss warranties (ILW). Lloyd's offers **London Bridge** as an ISPV for ILS capital. | |
| **Legacy / run-off** | Loss portfolio transfer (LPT), adverse development cover (ADC), RITC at Lloyd's. | |

**Renewal seasons [certain]:**

| Date | Market |
|---|---|
| 1 January | Main renewal for Europe, global programmes, much of London |
| 1 April | Japan, some Asia |
| 1 June | Florida property catastrophe |
| 1 July | US, Australia, New Zealand |

**Key reinsurance terms:** cedant, ceding commission, reinstatement premium, hours clause (defines an "event", e.g., 72 hours for windstorm), ultimate net loss (UNL), loss occurring during (LOD) vs risks attaching during (RAD), rate on line (ROL = premium ÷ limit), payback.

## 3. Placement method by class (typical) **[likely]**

| Class | Open market | Delegated authority | Facilities / consortia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marine hull | High | Low | Medium |
| Cargo | Medium | Medium (open covers, binders) | Medium |
| Aviation | High | Low | Low |
| Energy | High | Low | Medium |
| Large property D&F | High | Low | High (broker facilities) |
| US E&S small property | Low | High | Medium |
| Casualty – large US | High | Medium | Medium |
| Cyber SME | Low | High | Medium |
| Cyber large corporate | High | Low | Medium |
| A&H | Low | High | Low |
| Treaty reinsurance | High | Low | Low |

The split matters for consulting: **open market** problems are about broker–underwriter workflow, contract certainty and subscription; **delegated** problems are about bordereaux, oversight and data.

## 4. What clients worry about per class (conversation starters)

| Class | Questions you should ask a CUO |
|---|---|
| Property | "How confident are you in your SOV data quality and geocoding? What is your secondary peril (wildfire, convective storm, flood) view?" |
| Casualty | "How are you reflecting social inflation in pricing and reserves for older US years?" |
| Cyber | "How do you measure systemic aggregation across cyber and non-cyber classes (silent cyber)?" |
| Marine | "How do you track vessel movements against sanctions and listed areas in real time?" |
| Aviation | "What is your remaining exposure to the Russia lessor litigation and how is it reserved?" |
| Reinsurance | "With rates softening, how are you holding attachment points and terms?" |
| Specialty | "What is your view on emerging AI liability – exclusions, affirmative cover, or silent exposure?" |
