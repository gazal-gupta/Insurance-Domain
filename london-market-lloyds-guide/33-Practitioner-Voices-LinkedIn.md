## 1. Read this first: what LinkedIn evidence is worth

LinkedIn posts are **opinion, experience and marketing**. They are not verified facts. Most posts that came up in the search were written by vendors, recruiters or consultants selling something. Very few were first-hand accounts with numbers from underwriters or claims handlers.

I could not log in to LinkedIn. Six of the nine main articles returned "access denied" to automated reading. So each item below is graded:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Post text read in full |
| **B** | Article title and author known; content seen only as a search extract |
| **C** | Search extract only; author not confirmed – treat as market chatter |

**Rule for client work:** a LinkedIn view is a **hypothesis to test in interviews**, never a fact for a slide. Where a post makes a factual claim, the "Check against" column shows what the evidence in this guide says.

## 3. Data, MRC and the Core Data Record

| Grade | Voice | What they say | So what |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Kirstin Duffield (post, about 2 years old) | "The MRC v3 should be produced from data, not the other way around … Word is not the way to collect data." MRC v3 was designed to help extract data for firms not yet able to build contracts from data. Calls for a "stable yet flexible data model" that supports master data management. *Names vendor products – promotional* | The real goal is **data-first contracts**: build the contract from data. Today the market mostly extracts data from a Word document after the fact. Ask every client which way round they work |
| **A** | Kuba Matraj (post, about 3 years old) | If data quality is "not on the right level from the beginning it can bring lack of trust" and slow adoption. "Not every carrier would see benefits … straight away", so some will delay – "a big risk of staying behind". "Lots of vulnerable data will be captured", so security must be "on the highest possible level" | Early data quality decides whether people trust a standard. This is the Kinnect lesson again |
| **A** | Hélène Stanway (comment on the same post) | The CDR serves "3 use cases only": accounting and settlement, tax and regulatory reporting, and claims matching. It "does not replace the contract of insurance, the MRC" and "cannot complete until after bind" | A precise correction to over-selling the CDR. Note that the October 2026 extension has since widened the scope (Module 08) |
| B | James Livett – *A New MRC: Foundation for the Future* | The MRC "is more than just a data repository, it is the client policy". The CDR is delivered through iMRC infrastructure; the two are not the same thing | Supports the source guide's warning: MRC is not the whole wording, and the CDR is not the contract |
| C | Commentator | Brokers had to produce MRC v3-compliant contracts by 30 September 2023, which was short notice | Short adoption windows create work-arounds **[likely]** |

## 8. What the posts confirm, and what they add

**Confirms the guide [likely]:**
1. Market-wide big-bang programmes have lost credibility. Narrow standards and firm-level tools are the accepted path.
2. Data quality at source is the main blocker for DA, the CDR and AI.
3. Underwriting culture and change management limit AI more than technology does.

**Adds to the guide:**
1. **Data-first contracts** (build the MRC from data) is the practitioner's next ask beyond the CDR.
2. **Broker workload** is the hidden cost of DCOM and CDR; brokers will push back unless they also benefit.
3. **Continuous DA due diligence** changes staffing models.

**Gaps.** I found no credible infographic of the Lloyd's structure on LinkedIn; the posts were text or vendor graphics. The diagrams in Modules 02 and 21 are more accurate than anything found.

## 9. How to use LinkedIn as a consultant (weekly, 20 minutes)

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Follow **organisations**: Lloyd's, LMA, IUA, LIIBA, LMG, Velonetic, Lloyd's Lab, ACORD. Official announcements appear there first |
| 2 | Follow **roles, not celebrities**: heads of delegated authority, claims directors, active underwriters, COOs at managing agents. Search "Head of Delegated Authority Lloyd's" and choose people who post about their own work |
| 3 | Grade every post A, B or C as in this module, and note the vendor interest |
| 4 | Save one post a week into a "voice of the market" log with: claim, source, grade, test question |
| 5 | Turn the strongest claims into interview questions (Module 11 interview guide) |
| 6 | Post your own **so-what notes** (Module 19). Commenting with evidence builds your name faster than sharing articles |

**Do not:** quote LinkedIn numbers in a deliverable, name individuals' views to a client without permission, or take vendor posts as market evidence.
