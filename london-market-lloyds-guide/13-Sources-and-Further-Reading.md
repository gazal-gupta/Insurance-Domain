## 1. Self-assessment by level

Answer without looking. Model answers are short; if yours is weaker, re-read the module in brackets.

### Beginner (Modules 01–03)

| # | Question | Model answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why is Lloyd's not an insurance company? | It is a market where syndicates, run by managing agents and backed by members' capital, underwrite risk; the Corporation regulates and supports the market. |
| 2 | What are the four parts of the London market? | Lloyd's, company market, brokers, support services/infrastructure. |
| 3 | What is subscription? | One risk shared by many insurers; a lead sets terms, followers take shares. |
| 4 | Whose agent is the broker in the open market? | The insured's. |
| 5 | Name three lines where London is a global leader. | Marine, aviation, energy, cyber, political risk, reinsurance (any three). |
| 6 | What is the difference between treaty and facultative? | Treaty covers a portfolio automatically; facultative covers one risk negotiated individually. |
| 7 | What does a combined ratio of 92% mean? | For every £100 of earned premium, £92 is spent on claims and expenses – an underwriting profit of £8. |

### Intermediate (Modules 04–06)

| # | Question | Model answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name the six sections of the MRC. | Risk Details; Information; Security Details; Subscription Agreement; Fiscal and Regulatory; Broker Remuneration and Deductions. |
| 2 | Written lines total 125%. A 25% written line signs to? | 20%. |
| 3 | What does the GUA do? | Sets which contract changes the leader can agree alone and which need other agreement parties. |
| 4 | What are the three bordereaux types? | Risk, premium, claims. |
| 5 | What does SND stand for and why does it matter? | Signing Number and Date – the bureau reference that identifies each accounting entry and links premiums, endorsements and claims. |
| 6 | Who agrees a complex claim on a Lloyd's subscription contract? | The Lloyd's lead and second Lloyd's lead under the Lloyd's Claims Lead Arrangements (which replaced the Lloyd's Claims Scheme); followers are bound. |
| 7 | Give three causes of aged premium debt. | Late documentation, bureau rejections, DA remittance delays, client non-payment, disputes. |

### Advanced (Modules 07–09)

| # | Question | Model answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explain RITC. | At closure (normally 36 months), the closing year's members pay a premium to a later year/syndicate to take over remaining liabilities. |
| 2 | ECA formula? | uSCR × 135%. |
| 3 | What did Lloyd's decide about Blueprint Two in March 2026? | Retired the name and original vision; shift to incremental modernisation, common data standards (CDR retained) and refocused Velonetic. |
| 4 | What does PS25/21 change? | Larger commercial customers excluded from ICOBS, PROD and Consumer Duty; co-manufacturers can nominate a lead for PROD 4; MGAs cannot be lead. |
| 5 | Name Lloyd's four strategic drivers (2026–2030). | Leading underwriting performance; efficient and flexible marketplace; maximised capital advantage; a Lloyd's to be proud of. |
| 6 | Where does AI deliver most value today in London? | Submission intake and triage, wording checks, bordereaux processing, claims summarisation and triage. |
| 7 | What is the PRA's main GI concern in 2026? | Softening market leading internal models to overstate profitability and understate capital. |
